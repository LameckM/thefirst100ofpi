Learn the Major System

Five minutes. After this, any number becomes a handful of pictures.

1The big idea

Your brain is bad at digits and great at pictures. A long number like 314159 is hard to hold, but a mother holding a giant tulip is easy.

The Major System is a code that turns every digit into a consonant sound. String a few sounds together, add any vowels you like, and you get a word. Words become pictures, and pictures become a story you can replay to get the digits back.

3Four rules that trip people up

Sounds count, spelling does not. Say the word out loud. knee = 2 because the k is silent. phone = 82 because ph sounds like f. A c can go either way: city = 01 (soft c sounds like s) but cat = 71 (hard c sounds like k). Same for g: gem = 63 but go = 7.
Vowels, w, h and y are ignored. They only help you build a word. hen = 2, and wheat = 1.
A double letter that makes one sound counts once. ball = 95 and buzz = 90. But two separate sounds each count: mommy = 33.
Two letters that need care. ng counts as just n, so king = 72. And x is really k plus s, so it counts as two digits: box = 970.

5From a number to a story

Say you need to remember 314159. Split it into 314 and 159.

314 is m, th, r, which gives mother. 159 is t, l, p, which gives tulip. Now make a scene: your mother is holding a giant tulip. Two pictures, six digits.

To get the number back, see the scene, say the words, and decode each one: mother is 314, tulip is 159.

6Make it stick

Make every scene strange and physical. A normal mother holding a normal tulip fades. A mother the size of a bus swinging a flaming tulip does not.

For long numbers, walk a route you know well, like your house, and leave one scene at each stop. Walking the route in your head replays the digits in order.

Review the next day, then three days later, then a week later. Spacing it out is what moves it into long-term memory.
